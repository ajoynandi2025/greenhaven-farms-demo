LAND & LEGACY

Questions to ask before choosing a farm

A farm can represent a long-term relationship with a place, so it deserves careful thought. Before making a decision, look beyond the first impression and ask practical questions about the land, its management, and the commitments involved.

GREENHAVEN FIELD NOTES   ·   8 MIN READ

A farm can represent a long-term relationship with a place, so it deserves careful thought. Before making a decision, look beyond the first impression and ask practical questions about the land, its management, and the commitments involved.

Understand the land and its setting

Visit in different weather conditions if possible. Ask about road access, drainage, water availability, soil conditions, nearby land use, and any seasonal limitations. Spend time walking the property rather than relying only on photographs or a presentation.

Review documentation independently

Request the relevant title documents, survey plans, land-use permissions, approvals, and details of any shared facilities or easements. Have qualified local legal and property professionals review the paperwork. Requirements vary by location, and a sales conversation is not a substitute for independent advice.

Clarify ongoing costs and care

Ask what maintenance is included, who performs it, how often it happens, and what costs may arise separately. If there is a management agreement, read its duration, renewal terms, responsibilities, and exit provisions. Request clear written answers rather than relying on verbal assurances.

Think about your own purpose

Are you looking for a place to visit on weekends, a space to grow crops, a long-term family retreat, or simply an opportunity to learn about land stewardship? Your purpose affects what location, access, facilities, and level of involvement will suit you.

Look for transparent communication

A responsible provider should welcome reasonable questions and give you time to consider the decision. Be cautious of pressure to commit immediately or promises of guaranteed returns. Compare alternatives and take independent advice before signing or paying.

A thought to take with you

Use this checklist as a starting point, not as legal or financial advice. The right decision is one you understand clearly, including the responsibilities as well as the possibilities.
